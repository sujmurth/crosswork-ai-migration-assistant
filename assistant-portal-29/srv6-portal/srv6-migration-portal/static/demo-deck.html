The opportunity

Why move the VPN core to SRv6?

Radical simplification

One IPv6 data plane. Remove LDP and MPLS label distribution entirely — fewer protocols, fewer failure modes.

Native programmability

End-to-end SRv6 SIDs (uDT4 / uDX2) enable TE, slicing and per-service steering without a separate label plane.

Lower risk migration

Dual-plane coexistence, per-service cutover, and automated verification — no “big bang”.

The hard part isn’t SRv6 — it’s doing the migration safely, repeatably, and provably across a live fabric.

The MOP

Nine phases, fully orchestrated

0–1 · Prepare

Baseline capture & pre-checks — snapshot the working state.

2 · SRv6 locators

Provision locators (MAIN / TOP / BOTTOM / LATENCY).

3 · IS-IS SRv6

Enable SRv6 in the IGP, allocate uN / uA SIDs.

4 · BGP RRv6

Bring up IPv6 route-reflection to the RRv6 peer.

5 · L3VPN → SRv6

L3NM intent + per-VRF SRv6 locator (uDT4).

5b · L2VPN → SRv6

L2NM + ct-evpn-srv6 enabler (uDX2).

6–7 · Cutover

Remove mpls alloc → SRv6 becomes best path.

8 · Remove MPLS

Delete LDP / MPLS — SRv6-only data plane.

✓ · Verify

Automated SRv6 assertions on every PE.

Engineering that makes it real

Model-driven, with the right last-mile guardrails

Standards-based intent

Services migrated through IETF L3NM and L2NM — vendor-neutral, auditable, idempotent. TSDN custom-templates fill the gaps the models don’t render (e.g. the global evpn segment-routing srv6 enabler).

The finding that mattered

On this platform, leaving mpls alloc enable in place suppresses the SRv6-VPN SID at the ingress PE — traffic stays on MPLS. The real cutover is removing mpls-alloc, after which CEF programs SRv6 Headend.
